This policy covers Quotabell for iPhone, its widgets, the Mac helper and this website. Quotabell is made by LAZSOL AI PTE. LTD., Singapore. You do not create an account with LAZSOL. The apps have no LAZSOL servers, analytics, tracking, advertising or third-party crash-reporting SDKs. No app data is sent to LAZSOL.

What is stored where

On the Mac
Quota percentages, limit-window labels, reset times, plan labels, provider and account IDs, account nicknames, read status and timestamps are stored under ~/Library/Application Support/Quotabell/. This includes latest reads and local quota history. Successful quota history is pruned to 30 days when the helper starts. Some error details can remain locally; free-text error details are stripped from cloud sync.
Mac preferences and Wake
UserDefaults holds settings such as poll interval, launch preferences, account configuration, nicknames, optional CLI config-folder paths, a random per-Mac sync ID, alert preferences and the local alert ledger. Wake settings and consent are local; scheduler state and history are in Application Support. Wake records contain times, account name, outcome, fixed command arguments, before/after quota readings and CLI-reported token counts or cost when available. The history view filters to 60 days; the history file can remain until you remove it. Model reply text is not stored.
On the iPhone and in its App Group
The app and widgets share the App Group group.com.lazsol.quotabell. It contains cached Mac quota data (mac-state.json), phone-only quota readings and back-off state (phone-readings.json), and combined display data (merged-state.json). Shared preferences contain the account directory, nicknames, order, hidden-account choices, widget selections, onboarding and sample-data choices, source selections, phone-mode consent and the optional Keychain-sync preference. Notification settings and the alert ledger are local UserDefaults data.
In your private iCloud
The Mac helper syncs its quota state to your CloudKit private database in iCloud.com.lazsol.quotabell, one record per Mac. Besides quota numbers and reset times, this includes plan and window labels, provider/account IDs, nicknames, ordering/source metadata, sanitized read status, timestamps, a random app-generated Mac ID, the sanitized Mac name and summary Wake status (enabled, next/last time, outcome and pause/confirmation state). It does not sync provider credentials, conversations, CLI config-folder paths or the full local history. LAZSOL cannot read your private CloudKit database. Apple processes and stores this data under your iCloud arrangements.
In the iPhone Keychain
Only if you choose phone-only sign-in, Quotabell stores an account’s access/refresh tokens or fallback website-session cookie in its own Keychain items. The saved credential can include provider account identifiers or labels returned during sign-in. These items are device-only by default. If you explicitly enable the app’s “Sync phone sign-ins with iCloud Keychain” option, they become eligible for Apple’s iCloud Keychain sync. This is separate from CloudKit quota sync; tokens never go into CloudKit quota records.

Mac helper access

The helper asks the official Claude Code, Codex, Grok and Antigravity (agy) CLIs for usage, with a fixed allowlist of protocol messages. For Antigravity it runs the CLI’s built-in read-only usage report (agy -p /usage), which does not start an agent turn, and uses whichever Google account agy is currently signed into. It never reads or stores provider tokens, credential files or Keychain credentials. The CLIs authenticate themselves. For Claude, the helper can also read Claude.app’s plan-usage-history.json usage file as a fallback. For an account with a separate CLI config folder, Quotabell passes only the path as an environment variable to the CLI and does not open the folder; the Claude.app fallback is disabled for that account. Antigravity accounts on the Mac always use the current agy sign-in; config folders and Wake are not available for Antigravity.

Ordinary quota checks do not send prompts or read conversations. Optional Wake is the exception: it sends a fixed tiny prompt through your own Claude or Codex CLI, uses a small amount of your plan and offers up to three scheduled times a day per Mac account. Manual “Wake now” runs are additional. It is off until you enable it and accept its disclosure. The provider processes that message under its own terms and privacy policy.

Phone-only sign-in

Phone-only mode is optional. Its main sign-in flow uses Apple’s authentication sheet on the provider’s own page, presenting as the provider’s official CLI app (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Grok CLI or Antigravity CLI). Quotabell does not see your password in that flow. It receives tokens and uses them to read usage, refresh sign-in or request revocation, never to chat or run models on the phone.

The OAuth permissions can include identity and offline access; they are not necessarily a narrowly scoped, read-only usage permission. Grok may request the CLI’s broader scope on a subsequent sign-in after a refused usage read. For Antigravity, the Google sign-in uses Antigravity CLI’s own Google client and asks for Google Cloud platform access and your email address, which is broader than reading quota; the app explains this before you sign in. Quotabell uses it only for two fixed read-only quota requests and never to create projects, run models or chat. Review the actual permission screen. Sign-in responses may contain identity information; Quotabell does not send it to LAZSOL or put raw sign-in responses in quota caches.

This is not a provider-approved integration. As explained in the in-app disclosure, using official CLI identities and undocumented endpoints can breach provider rules or lead to account restrictions, particularly for Claude. Providers can change or block this access without notice. The Mac helper is the safer option.

The optional website fallback (Claude, ChatGPT / Codex and Grok only; Antigravity has none) opens the provider site in a non-persistent in-app web view and saves only the relevant session cookie as its credential in the Keychain. Passkeys and Google sign-in do not work in this fallback. Grok’s fallback opens an invisible, non-persistent grok.com web view for a same-origin usage read while the app is open; the provider page can load its own assets, including third-party assets. It shows Grok website chat limits, not the SuperGrok weekly pool.

Apple’s main sign-in sheet may use existing browser sessions and provider cookies according to its settings. Quotabell’s website itself sets no cookies. Provider websites and sign-in services have their own privacy policies.

Where requests go

Cloud sync goes to Apple’s CloudKit service in your private iCloud database. The Mac CLIs make their own provider requests. In phone-only mode, Quotabell’s credentialed requests are restricted to HTTPS on the relevant provider’s own allowlisted hosts, with cross-host redirects refused:

  • Claude: claude.ai, platform.claude.com, api.anthropic.com.
  • ChatGPT / Codex: auth.openai.com, chatgpt.com.
  • Grok: auth.x.ai, cli-chat-proxy.grok.com; grok.com for the website fallback. The provider’s sign-in sheet can also visit accounts.x.ai.
  • Antigravity CLI: accounts.google.com and oauth2.googleapis.com for sign-in, token refresh and revocation; cloudcode-pa.googleapis.com and daily-cloudcode-pa.googleapis.com for the read-only quota requests.

During a loopback sign-in, a temporary listener on the iPhone’s own 127.0.0.1 receives the redirect. It is not a LAZSOL server. Provider pages may direct you to your chosen identity provider to authenticate. Providers receive normal request information such as your IP address and process it under their own policies.

Notifications

Quota and reset notifications are generated locally on each device after a usage refresh. Alert preferences and a ledger prevent repeated warnings for the same window. They do not use a LAZSOL notification service. Separately, Apple’s CloudKit can use silent push to tell the iPhone to refresh synced data. Notifications can display provider names, account nicknames and quota details on your Lock Screen; control previews and permission in system settings.

Your choices and deletion

  • Phone sign-ins: use Sign Out for an account to delete its Keychain token and phone readings. If Keychain sync was enabled, the deletion also targets the synced item. Revocation is requested from OpenAI, xAI or Google on a best-effort basis; the Claude flow has no revocation endpoint, so deleting locally does not invalidate an issued token at the provider before it expires. Removing a phone account also signs it out.
  • Cloud quota data: use the iPhone app’s Setup & Status → Synced data deletion control to delete synced records and the Mac quota cache on that iPhone. This does not delete phone sign-ins, other preferences or the Mac’s local files. A running Mac helper can publish fresh records again; quit it first if you want syncing to stop.
  • Local Mac files: quit the helper and remove ~/Library/Application Support/Quotabell/ to remove its quota and Wake files. Mac preferences are separate system UserDefaults.
  • Permissions: turn off alerts, Wake or phone Keychain sync in the app, or disable iCloud access and notifications in system settings. Merely uninstalling the iPhone app should not be relied upon to delete Keychain or iCloud data; sign out and delete synced records first.

Latest readings and cloud records remain until replaced or deleted. Device backups, iCloud Keychain propagation and provider-side retention follow Apple’s and the providers’ policies. We cannot retrieve, edit or delete your private iCloud records on your behalf.

This website and support

This static site uses local fonts and assets, no analytics, no tracking, no cookies and no third-party embeds. It is intended to be hosted by Cloudflare, which necessarily processes connection information such as IP address, request URL and user agent to deliver and secure the site. Hosting logs and retention depend on the host’s configuration. There is no app-data upload through this site.

If you choose to contact support by email, LAZSOL receives the email address and information you send, solely to respond and handle the issue. Do not include passwords, tokens or full credential files. Keep screenshots and diagnostics free of personal or secret information. Support correspondence can be retained while needed to resolve the request and meet applicable obligations; you can request deletion of correspondence we hold.

Children

Quotabell is a utility for people with their own eligible provider accounts. It is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect children’s personal information. Follow the minimum-age requirements of Apple and your providers. If you believe a child has sent us personal information through support, contact us so we can address it.
